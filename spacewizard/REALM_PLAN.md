# The realm: expeditions, towns and harmony

The owner's plan for Space Wizard's long game, in three stages. Stage 1 is built. Stages 2 and 3 are design notes: agree the details with the owner before building (they like options first).

**The point of the whole game:** bring every land under your care and have them live in harmony, and spread true spirituality through the lands. Taking them by force or by trickery works, but leaves you a realm that won't obey.

## Stage 1: expeditions (built)

- **Ten gates** in the mountains round the edge of the home map. Each opens on a land of its own: you walk out of the home map and into a separate one. Three hold a city (Port Saltmere, Sandgate, Farlight), four a township (Reedham, Oakhollow, Cragfoot, Hearthwick), three are wild (the Blackfen, the Cinder Reach, the Thousand Isles).
- **The coach** takes you straight to any town or camp you've already reached on foot.
- **Bounties:** the city pays tokens for every beast you bring down. Twenty new beasts, two to a land.
- **Hired hands:** fighters and medics, hired at the Wayfarers' Guild in Hollowbrook and at the inns.
- **Food:** everyone eats a meal every half minute out in a far land. With none left you faint and wake at the gate.
- **Missions:** 34 of them, from folk in Hollowbrook and the far towns: letters, fetching, finding people, exploring, hunting named beasts, crying the news.
- **Reputation:** each mission pays it. Stage 2 spends it.

## Stage 2: running your town (to build)

Reputation buys you posts in Hollowbrook. A little reputation holds one post; more holds several. Doing a post well earns more reputation. **Each post's cards are the decisions that person would really face**, and each post has a budget that must balance.

- **Mayor.** You sit in council meetings where councillors propose things to build, and you choose. People in the private sector propose things too. Buildings include food production, weapons works, hospital supplies for the medics, and churches or temples.
  - No churches or temples: people start stealing; if it gets bad, revenge killings and burnt houses, driven by promiscuity and nihilism.
- **Dojo master.** In charge of the fighters; can raise an army. Choices: train more fighters, new fighting techniques, moral discipline.
  - No moral discipline: fighters are weaker, cowardly, fight among themselves, and are easily won over by others.
- **Head of the animal catchers.** Go out and catch beasts; decide which ones to train (training makes them stronger); raise an army that way. You can also trade with beasts.
- **Head of the university.** Scholars bring research ideas and programmes; you choose which to fund. Programmes improve weapons, healing (the medics get better by a set amount), and legal persuasion. You also choose which towns' students may study there, which opens trade with those towns.
  - No spiritual study: weapons and healing gain side effects that hurt your own people.
- **The arts** (at the university and in town). The more you spend, the better it gets, but what you fund matters:
  - Classically beautiful work scores highest. A faithful remake of a classic is good.
  - Now and then something new is better still: it uses the essence of the classics to make something that is itself a classic.
  - Merely modern, edgy or weird work scores low.
  - Arts alone attract visitors and invitations. Arts together with spirituality combine for a bigger effect on how well people live together.
- **Scores.** The town has a spirituality score, which raises everyone's effectiveness. Each citizen could have a social cohesion score from their exposure to spirituality, and to arts that carry it.

## Stage 3: between the towns (to build)

- **Trade** in food and cultural things. Deliveries are real: carts of food, visitors and performers walk the roads between towns. People come to watch or train at your dojo.
- **Missions of this kind:** go to a town and advertise your arts, your university or your dojo.
- **Armies and invasion.** Put an army together and march on another town.
- **Legal persuasion.** The persuasion team must get into the buildings; occupy every house and the town is persuaded, and everyone in it is yours to lead.
- **The other peoples.** Some are hostile to you, some to each other. They want land for their own reasons: food, arts, a belief that they're superior, fear of the others.
- **Alliances.** Others can ally with you and ask your help against a third. You can agree, and you can betray them, but betrayal lowers your trust with everyone.
- **Trust and obedience.** Lands taken by betrayal or force are harder to hold: you may command their dojos and universities, but people may not listen, and there may be coups.
- **Winning.** Every land under your care, living in harmony, with true spirituality spread through them all.
