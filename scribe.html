Space Wizard · a second way to cast

Scribing Dojo

Write a spell with a brush. The shape you draw is the spell, and how big it is, where it sits on the pad and where you pool the ink all count. Draw it a little off and it still works, only weaker. Draw it far off and nothing happens. When it takes, the ink lifts off the page and dances the spell.

Hold the brush still to pool ink. Lift, and wait a moment for the verdict.

The five styles

Each element writes like a fighting style moves. The glyphs are movements, not pictures: a stance, a turn, a kick. Every spell is that style's energy, danced out in front of you.

Where it comes from

  • The Tang calligrapher Zhang Xu is said to have found his wild cursive by watching Lady Gongsun's sword dance: writing as a dance on paper. (Zhang Xu, Grasping Calligraphy through the Sword Dance)
  • Brushwork is lifting and pressing: lift for thin strokes, press for thick ones, with pauses and weight at the ends and turns. Fast, dry strokes split into streaks called flying white. (Wang Xizhi's brush methods, brush techniques)
  • The ensō is a circle drawn in one stroke, in one breath, with no corrections. Left open it means growth; closed, completeness. (Ensō, Asian Art Museum)
  • In Shaolin's five animals, the dragon trains spirit, the snake energy, the tiger bones, the leopard strength and the crane essence. (Shaolin Wahnam)
  • Xingyi boxing has five element fists, among them a corkscrew drilling fist, a rising cannon fist and a fist that crosses the centre line; they shaped the Water, Fire and Earth glyphs. (The five elements and martial arts)
  • Aikido turns an attack aside in circles and spirals: irimi enters, tenkan turns. (Nippon Budokan, Iriminage)
  • Muay Thai fighters dance the Wai Kru Ram Muay round the ring before a fight; capoeira's half-moon kick swings round a planted foot; Hung Gar is built on a deep horse stance. (Wai Khru Ram Muay, Meia lua de compasso, Hung Ga)
  • Musashi, swordsman and ink painter, used the brush as he used the sword: directly, with no wasted motion. (Learning to See)
  • Pressure: a pen, and the few screens that sense it, report how hard you press; most phone screens don't, so the ink then follows speed and holding instead. (MDN: PointerEvent.pressure)
